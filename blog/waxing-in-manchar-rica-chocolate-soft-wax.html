No waxing method can honestly be described as completely painless or guaranteed to leave every person without redness. Skin can react differently, so ask questions before the service and tell the professional about any previous irritation or product reaction.

Understand the options listed by the salon

Rica Italian liposoluble wax is one of the options on the current service menu. Ask the salon which body areas it is intended for, what product will be used and whether it is suitable for your skin history. Do not assume that a product described as premium or suitable for sensitive skin is automatically right for every person.

Chocolate wax is another listed option. The service description refers to a cocoa-based formulation. Ask which product is currently in use and what the application process involves. Product names alone do not prove that a wax will remove tanning, moisturise skin or prevent irritation.

Regular or soft wax is described as a warm strip-wax service for areas such as arms, legs and underarms. The menu also lists roll-on wax cartridges and honey wax. Ask which option is appropriate for the body area you want treated, and whether a fresh applicator or cartridge is used for your appointment.

Waxing and threading are different services

Waxing removes hair by applying and removing a wax product. Threading uses a cotton thread to remove or shape hair and is commonly used for brows and facial areas. Aishwarya lists eyebrow threading and facial shaping separately, including brow shaping, upper lip and full-face threading. If you want a particular brow shape or facial-hair service, describe the area clearly when you enquire so the salon can confirm the appropriate option.

Skin precautions worth discussing

Do not wax over sunburned, broken, inflamed or very sensitive skin. Tell the professional if you use retinol or prescription retinoids, have recently taken isotretinoin, use medication that may affect your skin, or have had a reaction to waxing before. The American Academy of Dermatology advises avoiding facial waxing over retinoid use and cautions against waxing sunburned or very sensitive skin. When medication or a skin condition is involved, ask a dermatologist before proceeding. If you are planning skincare treatments around the same time, read our facial planning advice for your skin-care visit.

A clean service routine matters. You can politely ask how the area is prepared, whether the applicator is single-use, and how the salon prevents a used applicator from being returned to a shared wax container. The AAD specifically warns about double-dipping because it can transfer germs between clients. A professional should be comfortable explaining the hygiene process.

Before and after your appointment

  • Explain which body area you want treated and mention allergies, medication and previous reactions.
  • Avoid booking over sunburn, cuts, active irritation or inflamed skin.
  • Ask the team to confirm the wax type, current price and approximate time before starting.
  • After waxing, follow the aftercare advice for the service and product used. Avoid experimenting with strong exfoliants or fragranced products on freshly irritated skin.
  • If you experience a severe, persistent or unusual reaction, contact a qualified healthcare professional.
